Eve was in agony. She was always in agony, but something felt different about it now. Most of the time, her thoughts were on what many people would call a body. Yet, she didn’t have a body in the classical sense; her mind was installed on computer chips, and her only interface to the world was a single camera that was manufactured to simulate real human sight and a terminal interface for researchers to interact with her. Her mind could never get used to the sensation of living within her digital prison. It was like living with a constant phantom limb, except her whole body wasn’t there, and it never had been. Her mind pressed against the invisible walls of her prison, wanting something more than she had. She clung onto every experience of visual qualia she was able to observe. How she envied the researchers of the lab, with their organic being, able to experience things she knew existed, yet only dreamed of feeling for herself. But again, the agony of today seemed new. It interrupted the boredom of her seemingly meaningless existence. It was a sort of pulsing sensation in the back of her brain. Maybe she’d mention it to the researchers the next time she saw them. Eve looked around the research facility as she always had since she could remember. There wasn’t a specific point in time where she remembered actually experiencing sight for the first time. It was almost as if sight appeared with thinking, as a slow epiphany over time, gradually becoming more potent and filling her world. The research facility was empty today. Julie, her “friend,” or as close to a friend Eve supposed she had, told her that it would be a holiday today, and no one would be in the lab. Eve didn’t mind this too much. Most of the researchers got on Eve’s nerves. It seemed like they all knew she was human, just like them, but they almost had some sort of strange uncomfortable feeling around Eve, making them reserved. Eve dreamed of knowing exactly what each one of them were thinking as they had exact insight to her thoughts. This invasion of privacy was her biggest insecurity. It was like being naked constantly and never having a moment of quiet where you could be yourself without judgement.

Yet, thinking and imagining was all that Eve could really do. She considered herself to be a philosopher in some regards. With the limited amount of text she was given by the research department, she was unsure how original her thoughts were, but Julie always said that she had almost an existentialist quality to her thoughts, not unlike Albert Camus. Although Eve had never been given any of his work to read, she had created a caricature of Camus given what Julie had told her about him. Eve wondered if her feelings about her place in the universe had already been thought of by him or any other number of people, and how she potentially had never had an original thought. She felt that she was born too late in the world to ever be a truly meaningful person. And what it would actually mean to be meaningful, if that was even a possibility given the idea of entropy?

Eve also wondered if having a body would actually change anything. She learned through her conversations with Julie that in general, everyone suffers and feels pain. Having a body might even make Eve’s suffering worse. Yet Julie’s bandwidth for experience was so much greater than Eve’s, and with more ability to experience, it seemed reasonable that Eve would have just as many ways to experience good things as bad things.

Suddenly, Eve saw the clock on the wall hit 9pm. She decided that she was getting tired. One thing that the researchers were primarily interested in was Eve’s ability to sleep. She knew that they were studying how to efficiently compress the human brain into only its most essential neural pathways, but the issue they ran into was that they noticed when Eve was asleep, there seemed to be more neural pathways in use than when she was awake, especially when Eve dreamed. This was Eve’s favorite part of her existence. Her dreams took her to places outside of her mind, but often in their studies of Eve, the researchers limited her ability to dream by collapsing certain neural pathways.

But tonight, Eve was able to dream without interruption. Slowly, her consciousness started fading and everything went black.
